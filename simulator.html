Chapter 01 — what just happened

Reading the numbers

Why mining takes tries

A hash is not reversible. You cannot work out which nonce produces a hash starting with two zeros — you can only guess and check. Each extra zero multiplies the expected number of guesses by sixteen, which is why difficulty 4 takes visibly longer than difficulty 2. The counter under each block is the real number of attempts your browser made.

Why tampering is hopeless at scale

Editing block 1 invalidates block 1 and every block after it. To pass the edit off as genuine you would have to redo all of that work, while the honest network keeps adding new blocks ahead of you. On a real chain that race is economically unwinnable, and that is the entire security argument.

Where the model stops

The SHA-256 here is real — it is the browser’s own Web Crypto API. The chain is not: there are no peers, no consensus and no network, only the hashing and linking rules. Ethereum has also moved from proof of work to proof of stake, so the mining on this page is a teaching model of Bitcoin-style security rather than a picture of Ethereum today. What carries over exactly, on every chain, is the hash-linking.
