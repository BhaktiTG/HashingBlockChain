Chapter 00 — four comparisons

Four pairs worth telling apart

None of these is a contest. Each one is a trade — you give something up to get something else. The useful knowledge is knowing which trade, and when it is worth making.

01 · Web2 vs Web3 02 · Ethereum vs Bitcoin 03 · Public vs private key 04 · Blockchain vs database

01Web2 vs Web3

who holds the record

Web2

  • Your account is a row in a company’s database, and the company decides what that row means.
  • You sign in with a password the platform can reset, suspend or delete.
  • Fast and cheap, because one operator runs the whole stack and answers to nobody.
  • The rules can change overnight. An API you built on can close without warning.

Web3

  • Your balance lives on a ledger that thousands of independent machines hold copies of.
  • You sign in with a keypair you hold. Nobody can reset it — including you.
  • Slow and costly, because that same work is repeated everywhere for verification.
  • The rules are contract code: public, permanent, and hard to change unilaterally.

So what? Web3 buys credible neutrality and pays for it in speed, cost and personal responsibility. Worth it when the thing at stake is ownership. Rarely worth it for a photo feed.

02Ethereum vs Bitcoin

what the chain is for

Bitcoin

  • Built to be sound money: hold value, move value, resist change.
  • Deliberately limited scripting — fewer features means fewer ways to break.
  • Proof of work: security is bought with electricity and hardware.
  • Hard cap of 21 million coins, with issuance halving on a fixed schedule.

Ethereum

  • Built to be a programmable settlement layer: run applications, not just payments.
  • Turing-complete contracts in the EVM — lending, exchanges, NFTs, rollups.
  • Proof of stake since The Merge: security is bought with staked ETH that can be slashed.
  • No hard cap; issuance is low and a share of every fee is burned under EIP-1559.

So what? Bitcoin optimises for not changing. Ethereum optimises for what you can build. Both rely on the same hash-linking this site demonstrates — they differ in what they let you put inside the blocks.

03Public key vs private key

how ownership is proved

Public key

  • Derived from the private key; your address is derived from it in turn.
  • Safe to share. It is how people pay you and how anyone identifies you on-chain.
  • Used by the network to verify that a signature really came from you.
  • Reveals your whole transaction history, because the ledger is public.

Private key

  • A secret number generated first. Everything else is computed from it.
  • Never shared. Whoever holds it owns the funds, and there is no appeal above it.
  • Used to sign, proving authorisation without ever exposing the key itself.
  • Cannot be recovered if lost. Cannot be revoked if stolen.

So what? The maths runs one way only — public from private, never the reverse. That asymmetry is the entire security model, which is why a seed phrase in a screenshot is the most common way people lose everything.

04Blockchain vs traditional database

who can rewrite history

Traditional database

  • An administrator can UPDATE or DELETE any row, and often nobody outside would know.
  • One owner, one source of truth, trusted because of the institution behind it.
  • Extremely fast and cheap. Millions of writes per second is unremarkable.
  • Optimised for correct current state. History is a backup, not a guarantee.

Blockchain

  • Append-only. Each block commits to the hash of the last, so edits cascade and show.
  • Replicated across many independent nodes that must agree on the same history.
  • Slow and expensive on purpose — the redundancy is the product, not the overhead.
  • Optimised for verifiable history. Anyone can re-check every change from genesis.

So what? Reach for a blockchain only when someone silently editing the record would cost more than all that redundancy. Prove the difference yourself on the Block Simulator.
